How to Get into a German University for Your Masters

A practical guide based on a real application cycle that resulted in acceptances from multiple German universities across AI, Machine Learning, and Data Science programs. Written for Indian students applying to Masters programs in Computer Science and related fields.

Written by an Indian CS graduate who applied to 12+ German universities for Winter 2026/27 and navigated APS, VPD, direct applications, and everything in between. The most important thing to know upfront: your prerequisites are everything. If your bachelors coursework does not match what the university requires, you will not get in, no matter how strong the rest of your application looks.

Part I Understanding the Landscape
This guide is for Masters, not Bachelors

Everything here applies to Masters (M.Sc.) applications at German public universities. The bachelors process is different and not covered here. German Masters programs in CS and AI are genuinely strong, mostly free, and absolutely achievable for a well-prepared Indian applicant. The challenge is navigating a system that is unfamiliar and full of steps that are easy to miss.

The author of this guide studied Computer Science and applied to over a dozen German universities for programs in Machine Learning, AI, Data Science, and CS. The guide is built directly from that experience.

The single most important thing: your prerequisites. German university admissions are largely decided by whether your bachelors coursework maps onto what they require. Theory of Computation, Algorithms, Mathematics, Programming. If those are not on your transcript, you will likely be rejected before anyone even reads your motivation letter. Check prerequisites before you apply to anything.
Two types of applications
Type A
Direct Application
You apply straight through the university's own portal. No third-party document verification needed. Simpler, faster, and free to apply. Examples: University of Bonn, Göttingen, Augsburg, Freiburg, Hamburg, Heidelberg.
Type B
uni-assist / VPD Required
You must first submit documents to uni-assist, a centralized verification service, which issues a VPD that you then attach to your university application. Examples: TU Berlin, FU Berlin, TU Dresden. Costs around €95 per university and takes 4 to 8 weeks.

Your shortlist will likely have a mix of both. Direct-application universities are simpler to handle first. VPD universities require you to start the uni-assist process months before the university's own deadline, so plan well ahead.

Tuition and costs

Most German public universities charge no tuition fees. You pay a semester contribution (Semesterbeitrag) of roughly €300 to €400, which usually includes a public transport pass for the city. The exception is universities in Baden-Württemberg state (Freiburg, Heidelberg, Karlsruhe), which charge €1,500 per semester for non-EU students on top of the semester fee. Still very low compared to most alternatives.

The 99% rejection warning

Several German universities explicitly state that over 99% of applications from Indian students are rejected. This sounds alarming but it points to a very specific problem: most Indian applicants do not meet the prerequisites. Common gaps include missing Theory of Computation, a final year project that reads like a software report rather than a scientific thesis, and transcripts without enough mathematics coverage. The rejection rate is not a reflection of talent. It is a mismatch between what many Indian B.Tech programs cover and what German research universities require. If your coursework is strong and your documents are in order, you have a real shot.

Part II When to Do What
The full timeline for Winter Semester (starts October)

The Winter Semester starts in October and is the main intake for Masters programs. Most non-EU application windows open between February and June of the same year. Here is how the full cycle looks based on the real experience this guide draws from.

Aug – Sep
(year before)
Build your university shortlist. Research programs, check prerequisites against your transcript, and note which universities require VPD and which accept direct applications. A spreadsheet tracking each university, its deadline, portal type, and required documents is genuinely useful here.
October
(year before)
Apply for your APS certificate straight away. The APS is mandatory for all Indian applicants to German universities and takes time. In the cycle this guide is based on, the APS was submitted around October 22 and received on November 5, roughly two weeks. Start earlier to be safe, as timelines can vary. See Part IV for exactly what you need.
Nov – Jan
Prepare your core documents. Write your CV in German academic format (reverse chronological, no gaps over three months, countries of residence listed). Draft your motivation letter template. Gather all semester transcripts, properly stamped and scanned. Book and take your TOEFL if you have not already. Aim for 95+ iBT; some programs require 100+ for C1 level.
Feb – Mar
Early application windows open. Heidelberg (Feb 1 to Mar 15), Hamburg (Feb 15 to Mar 31), and several others open portals in this window. Apply as soon as the portal opens. Do not wait until the deadline. Some programs review on a rolling basis and fill up.
Early to Mid April
Submit VPD applications to uni-assist for any universities that require it (TU Berlin, FU Berlin, TU Dresden, and others). In the experience this guide draws from, VPDs were submitted in early to mid April and all three were received on May 27. Give yourself at least 6 to 8 weeks before the university portal deadline.
Apr – May
Mid-cycle application windows. Göttingen, Freiburg, Augsburg, and Bonn portals are typically open during this period. Complete your prerequisite mapping documents for each university and customize your motivation letter, especially the professor paragraph.
Late May – Jul
VPDs arrive, then apply to VPD universities immediately. TU Dresden's portal opens June 1 with a July 15 deadline. TU Berlin runs roughly May through mid-July. FU Berlin's deadline is May 31. Note that RWTH Aachen has an unusually early deadline (around March 15) so that one needs to be handled much earlier.
Jul – Sep
Decisions arrive. Some universities notify by July 15 (Bonn targets this for non-EU applicants). Others take until August or September. Offers usually come with an acceptance deadline of a few weeks. Apply for your student visa as soon as you accept an offer, since it takes 8 to 12 weeks.
On acceptance
Apply for student accommodation immediately. Dorm waiting lists in German university cities run months long. As soon as you have any offer, apply to the Studentenwerk (student services organization) for that city. Do not wait until you have decided which university to attend. Apply in every city you have an offer from. See Part VI for more on this.

THESIS

MODULE BOOK
University module handbook / official course syllabus — the document from your university describing what each course covers. Download it from your university's website. You will need it to support your prerequisite mapping, and some universities explicitly ask you to upload it.
EXTRAS
Internship certificates, research papers, conference letters — upload these as additional documents where allowed. A published or accepted paper at a strong venue carries significant weight. Internship certificates should document dates, tasks, and duration.
Part IV APS and VPD — The India-Specific Steps
The APS certificate

The APS (Akademische Prüfstelle, or Academic Evaluation Centre) certificate is mandatory for every Indian applicant to any German university. It is issued by the German Embassy in New Delhi and verifies that your academic documents are genuine. Without it, your application will be rejected at the initial screening stage regardless of how strong your profile is.

Apply for APS as early as possible. In the best case it takes around two weeks (submitted October 22, received November 5 in the real cycle this guide covers). It can take longer. If you are targeting Winter Semester, aim to have your certificate by November at the latest. This is not something to leave for February.
1
Register on the APS India website
Go to aps-india.de and create an account. Start your application there. Your name and date of birth must match your passport exactly — any mismatch causes delays.
2
Upload your documents and pay the fee
Upload scanned originals of your 10th and 12th marksheets (not Digilocker downloads), all university transcripts semester by semester, bonafide certificate, NAAC certificate, TOEFL score, and student authorization letter. Pay the APS fee and upload the payment proof.
3
Attend the interview at the German Embassy
You will be called for an in-person interview at the German Embassy in New Delhi or the nearest consulate. Bring all original documents. The interview is a verification process checking that what you submitted matches what you have in hand.
4
Receive your APS certificate
Once verified you receive your APS certificate. Store it carefully. You will need it for every German university application you submit, including the VPD process.
The VPD (for uni-assist universities)

The VPD (Vorprüfungsdokumentation) is a pre-evaluation document issued by uni-assist. For universities that require it, you must submit your documents to uni-assist first, receive the VPD, then attach it to your university portal application. You cannot skip this step.

Cost
Around €95 per university
A one-time uni-assist membership fee of around €20 plus roughly €75 per university. Only apply for VPDs at universities you are seriously considering.
Time
4 to 8 weeks
In the real application this guide covers, VPDs were submitted in early to mid April and received on May 27 for all three universities. Apply early to give yourself buffer before university portal deadlines.
1
Create a uni-assist account and pay the membership fee
Go to uni-assist.de and create an account. Pay the one-time membership fee, then add each university you need a VPD for and pay the per-university fee.
2
Upload your documents
Submit your APS certificate, all semester transcripts, 10th and 12th marksheets, TOEFL score, enrollment certificate and fee receipt, and passport copy. Some universities (TU Dresden for example) also require you to complete an aptitude questionnaire within the uni-assist system where you map your courses to required subject areas.
3
Wait for uni-assist to process
uni-assist reviews your documents and converts your GPA to the German grading scale. A CGPA of around 8.2/10 typically converts to roughly 1.8 on the German 1.0 to 5.0 scale. The VPD they issue reflects this conversion.
4
Receive the VPD and apply to the university
Once you receive your VPD, go to the university's own application portal and complete your application there. Upload the VPD alongside your other documents. The university will not process applications without it.
VPD validity: The VPD must not be older than one year at the time of application. If you have a VPD from a previous cycle, check whether it is still valid.
Converting your GPA to the German scale

German universities use a scale from 1.0 (best) to 5.0 (fail), where 4.0 is the minimum passing grade. To understand where you stand, use the Modified Bavarian Formula, which is the standard used for Indian applicants.

Formula: German Grade = 1 + 3 × ((Maximum Grade - Your Grade) / (Maximum Grade - Minimum Passing Grade))

Example (10-point scale, CGPA 8.23, passing grade 4.0): 1 + 3 × ((10.0 - 8.23) / (10.0 - 4.0)) = 1.885, rounds to 1.9 — Gut (Good).

Part V The Application Itself
Prerequisites — the heart of your application

This cannot be overstated: your prerequisites determine whether you get in. Every German Masters program in CS, AI, or Data Science has a list of required subject areas with minimum credit thresholds. If your transcript does not cover them, you are rejected at the eligibility screening stage before anyone reads your motivation letter or sees your publications.

For most CS and AI programs the required areas look something like this, though exact requirements vary by university and you should always check the specific program page:

Subject AreaTypical MinimumWhat counts
Algorithms and Theory14 ECTSDSA, Design and Analysis of Algorithms, Theory of Computation, Discrete Mathematics, Automata Theory
Programming and Software20 ECTSProgramming courses, Software Engineering, labs and practicals
Mathematics18 ECTSCalculus, Linear Algebra, Probability and Statistics, Numerical Methods
Bachelor's Thesis12 ECTSMust be a scientific thesis framing a research question — not a project report

Before applying anywhere, sit down with your transcript and build a prerequisite mapping document: a table with each required area, which of your courses covers it, and the credit count. Many portals ask you to enter this directly, in what is called the "technical qualifications" section or "aptitude test." This is not optional and it is not a formality. Take it seriously.

ECTS conversion for Indian credits: Most Indian universities do not use ECTS. As a general rule, 1 Indian credit (one lecture hour per week per semester) is roughly equivalent to 1.5 ECTS. Apply this consistently across all fields when mapping your courses. Some portals ask for your university's own credits rather than ECTS, in which case enter your numbers as-is and note the system in the comments field.
How prerequisite entry looks in a portal

Many portals ask you to enter courses in a specific text format, one per line. It typically looks like this:

Example format — Technical Qualifications section
Module name;year completed;credit hours;grade;; Design and Analysis of Algorithms;2025;4;9;; Theory of Computation;2024;4;8;; Discrete Mathematics;2024;4;8;; Probability Statistics and Linear Programming;2024;4;7;; Software Engineering;2025;3;8;;

You can also split a course across two categories if it genuinely covers both areas. For example, Applied Mathematics I might count toward both Analysis and Linear Algebra — enter it in each category with proportional credits. This is permitted and encouraged.

The motivation letter

The motivation letter is the most important document you write yourself. It is usually entered directly into the portal with a character limit (around 4,000 characters at Bonn, for example) rather than uploaded as a file.

A strong letter covers five things: your research narrative (what you have been working on and what you found), your academic foundation (how your coursework prepared you for this), why this specific program at this specific university, which professors you want to work with and why, and your longer-term goals. The professor paragraph is the most important part. Go to the university's CS and AI faculty page, find one or two professors whose work connects to yours, read a recent paper from each, and write about why their research connects to what you have been doing. Generic letters that could apply to any university get filtered quickly.

When applying to multiple universities, keep your core narrative the same but rewrite the professor paragraph entirely for each one, update the program name throughout, and swap any university-specific references (each university has its own research centers and institutes worth mentioning).
The CV (Lebenslauf)

German academic CVs follow a specific format. Every period from secondary school to now must be accounted for with no gap longer than three months. Entries go in reverse chronological order. Countries of residence must be listed explicitly. Describe what you achieved rather than just listing responsibilities.

Include
What to put in
All education with exact dates, research internships (name the supervisor and institution), industry internships with role and tasks, publications with venues, awards and scholarships, open-source projects with links, and all countries where you have lived.
Leave out
What to skip
A photo (not needed on German academic CVs), vague role descriptions, unexplained timeline gaps, and anything that cannot be verified. No "hardworking team player" type language.
The Expected Completion Form

If you have not yet graduated when you apply, most universities require an Expected Completion of Studies form stamped by your examination office. The form typically has three options: all requirements fulfilled and the certificate is being issued, all exams passed but administrative steps are pending, or exams are not yet fully completed with remaining work stated as a fraction of one full year of study.

If you have sat your final exams but results are not yet declared, tick option 3. If results are declared but your degree certificate has not been issued, tick option 2. The remaining workload is expressed as a fraction of one year — for a university where one year is roughly 52 credits, half a semester remaining would be written as 26/52.

Dealing with your examination office: Many Indian examination offices are unfamiliar with this form. When you go to get it stamped, attach the form's own instructions and specifically point them to the footnote that says "use your university's own credit system." This usually resolves any confusion about what numbers to fill in.
What makes a strong application
HIGHEST WEIGHT
Academic publications. A first-authored paper at a top venue like ICML, NeurIPS, AAAI, ACL, ICLR, or CVPR is transformative for your application. Even a paper at a solid workshop or regional venue helps significantly. Upload every paper you have, including preprints and work that has been accepted but not yet published.
HIGH
GPA and mathematics coverage. GPA matters but is not decisive alone. Strong scores in advanced mathematics (probability, statistics, linear algebra, analysis) and theory courses carry significant weight.
HIGH
Research internships. International research experience with a named faculty supervisor is valued. Document these carefully with certificates showing dates, tasks, and outcomes.
MEDIUM
Scientific thesis. A bachelors thesis that frames a research question, reviews prior literature, presents a methodology and results, and draws conclusions. This is meaningfully different from a project report. Some universities weight this as heavily as publications.
MEDIUM
Extracurricular achievements. Scholarships, competitions, open-source contributions, conference presentations (even without a full paper), tutoring and TA experience.
USEFUL
TOEFL above the minimum. A score of 100+ or IELTS 7.5+ signals strong communication ability and helps when programs are reviewing many borderline applications.
Common rejection reasons
Reason 1
Missing theory courses
No Theory of Computation, insufficient Discrete Mathematics, or weak algorithms coverage disqualifies many applicants at eligibility screening before anyone reviews the rest of the application.
Reason 2
Thesis is a project report
A final year project that builds an application or deploys a system without framing a research question in the context of existing literature is not considered a scientific thesis.
Reason 3
Wrong or missed deadline
Non-EU applicants often have an earlier deadline than domestic applicants at the same university. Missing it by even one day means automatic rejection regardless of how strong the application is.
Applying to multiple universities

Applying to several German universities at once makes good sense. Direct-application universities cost nothing to apply to, deadlines often overlap, and you can make your final decision after seeing all offers. A reasonable approach is one or two strong targets, two or three solid mid-tier options, and two or three reliable choices. Keep a spreadsheet with every university, its portal type, deadline, documents required, and current submission status. Managing twelve applications without one is genuinely difficult.

Some portals let you apply to multiple programs at the same university. At Bonn, for example, you can apply to AI, CS, and Cyber Security. Most fields are shared across programs, but you submit a separate application for each with a customized motivation letter. Be careful: once you submit one application at Bonn, shared fields are locked. Fill everything out completely before your first submission.

Part VI Dorms, Visa, and After Acceptance
Apply for student housing immediately

Student accommodation (Studentenwohnheime) in German university cities is managed by the Studentenwerk for each region. Demand is high and waiting lists are long — sometimes several months. As soon as you receive any offer of admission, apply to the Studentenwerk for that city even if you have not yet decided whether to accept. If you have offers from multiple cities, apply in all of them. You can cancel later. It costs nothing to register, and it protects your options.

The student visa

German student visas (Visum zu Studienzwecken) take 8 to 12 weeks from application to approval. Book your appointment at the German Embassy or Consulate in India straight away after accepting an offer — appointment slots fill up fast.

Admission letter from the university, on official letterhead with stamp.
Blocked account (Sperrkonto) with at least the required amount (around €11,208 for 2024/25 — verify the current figure). Fintiba, Expatrio, and Deutsche Bank are common options for students.
Health insurance — German public health insurance (gesetzliche Krankenversicherung). TK, AOK, and Barmer are popular choices for students. You can arrange this in advance of arriving.
Valid passport with at least two years of validity remaining.
Accommodation proof — a dorm acceptance letter or rental agreement. Applying for Studentenwerk housing early is partly so you have this document in time for your visa appointment.
Biometric photos and completed visa application form from the German Embassy website.

A final note

The German Masters application process is manageable once you understand its structure. The paperwork feels overwhelming at first because there are so many moving parts and the terminology is unfamiliar. But the actual work is straightforward if you start early, track everything in a spreadsheet, and treat the prerequisite mapping as seriously as you would any important exam.

The author of this guide, a Computer Science graduate from India, applied to over a dozen German universities and received admissions to multiple programs in Machine Learning, AI, and Data Science. The process works if you work the process.
